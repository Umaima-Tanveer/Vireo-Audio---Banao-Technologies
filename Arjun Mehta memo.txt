# Vireo Audio — Refund Review

**To:** Arjun Mehta, Finance Controller
**Subject:** Reconciled support-ticket refunds — Jan 2025 to Jun 2026

## Executive finding

The support export contains **12,238 ticket rows**, including migrated records and duplicate ticket IDs across the current helpdesk and legacy system. After reconciling the two sources and normalizing the legacy monetary values, the dataset contains **2,340 canonical refund tickets worth ₹67.10 lakh**.

This provides a reproducible baseline for monthly refund reporting by **reason code, agent, and team**.

## What requires review

The analysis produces a defined review queue rather than treating every exception as a confirmed policy issue:

* **879 GW-OTHER refunds exceed the ₹500 goodwill-credit cap**, representing **₹28.72 lakh**.
* **166 tickets contain both a refund and replacement**, representing **₹5.74 lakh**. Policy says a refund and replacement should not both be issued for the same order without escalation.
* **145 order links are ambiguous** after fallback matching and should be checked before relying on the related order information.
* **110 refunds exceed the listed product retail price**, representing **₹7.58 lakh**. These may be legitimate where quantities or other order-level circumstances explain the amount.

These figures are **review flags, not confirmed policy violations or recoverable savings**.

## Data controls

The reconciliation identified **638 ticket IDs appearing across both helpdesk and legacy systems**. Comparable duplicate records showed the legacy monetary values at 100× the corresponding helpdesk values, so legacy amounts were normalized before aggregation. Current helpdesk records were retained when the same ticket appeared in both systems.

The final refund total reconciles across the monthly and agent reports with a **₹0 difference**.

## Recommended next steps

1. Finance should use the **2,340-ticket / ₹67.10 lakh reconciled dataset** as the current reporting baseline.
2. Review the **879 GW-OTHER > ₹500 records**, starting with approval and order context rather than assuming an exception is incorrect.
3. Investigate the **166 refund-plus-replacement records** and the **145 ambiguous order matches**.
4. Add a reliable **`refund_processed_at`** field to future exports so monthly reporting uses the actual refund date rather than `created_at`.

**Note:** The prototype's AI assistant is used only for ticket-text quality control. Financial totals and reporting are calculated deterministically from the reconciled data.
